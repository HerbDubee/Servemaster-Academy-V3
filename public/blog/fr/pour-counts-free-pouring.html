Pour Counts & Free Pouring: Accuracy Without a Jigger

Free pouring looks effortless when done well — and exposes a bartender immediately when done poorly. Learning to count pours accurately protects your bar's profits and keeps every drink consistent.

Free pouring — pouring spirits without measuring each pour with a jigger — is a common technique in high-volume bar environments. When done accurately, it speeds service and looks polished. When done inaccurately, it erodes profits (over-pouring) or creates inconsistent cocktails and guest complaints (under-pouring). Most bartenders who think they free pour accurately actually don't. The test is simple, and it should become a regular habit.

How pour counting works

Pour counting uses a mental count to measure the flow of liquid from a speed pourer. The standard rule of thumb used in most North American bars: a count of 4 (roughly 4 seconds at a steady pour) delivers approximately 30ml (1 oz). A count of 6 delivers approximately 45ml (1.5 oz). This is only true if your speed pourers are calibrated and your pour angle is consistent.

  • Count "one-and-two-and-three-and-four" — the "and" beats slow the count to match the actual flow rate rather than rushing through numbers
  • Hold the bottle at 90 degrees to the floor — tilting the bottle less than fully inverted slows the flow and makes your count inaccurate
  • Use consistent speed pourers — different brands flow at different rates; mixed pourers on a bar create inconsistency even with perfect counting
  • Test your pour weekly — pour a count of 4 into a jigger and confirm it reads 30ml; drift happens gradually and goes unnoticed without testing

Calibrating your free pour

Before your shift — particularly when starting at a new bar — test your pour. Fill a bottle with water. Free pour a count of 4 into a jigger. If it reads consistently at 30ml, your pour is calibrated. If it reads over 35ml, you're over-pouring. Under 25ml, you're under-pouring. Adjust your count or your bottle angle accordingly.

Perform this test across different bottles, as bottle neck shapes vary. A narrow-necked spirit bottle flows differently than a wide-necked liqueur. Know your bar's specific bottles and calibrate to each one.

"A bartender who over-pours by just 5ml per drink costs a busy bar thousands of dollars a year. Accuracy is not pedantry — it is professionalism."

When to use a jigger regardless

Free pouring is a speed tool, not a precision tool. There are specific situations where a jigger is the correct choice regardless of your free pour skill:

  • Complex, multi-ingredient cocktails where balance is critical — a 5ml variance in a Negroni is noticeable
  • Premium and expensive spirits where overpouring has significant cost implications
  • When learning a new cocktail recipe during its first few services
  • During any period when you've been away from the bar for extended time — your calibration drifts without regular practice

The jigger debate: speed vs. precision

Many experienced craft bartenders jigger every drink, every time — not because they can't free pour, but because they choose not to. The argument is sound: a precisely measured drink is consistently better. The counter-argument is that at high volume, jiggering slows service to the point where guests wait unacceptably. Know your venue's standard and match it — and if no standard exists, advocate for one.

Building the habit of accuracy

Pour accuracy is a muscle. Bartenders who test themselves regularly maintain it; those who don't, gradually drift. Build a test into your pre-shift routine at least twice a week. Keep a small note of your results over time — most bartenders are surprised by how much their pour drifts across the course of a busy month. Accuracy, practiced consistently, becomes automatic. And automatic accuracy is one of the markers that distinguishes a competent bartender from an excellent one.
