Sofia's sneakers hit the damp cobblestones in steady cadence, the air still cool and heavy with the night's mist rolling off the Arno. Dawn broke slow over Florence, painting the Ponte Vecchio's arches in faint pink. She ran the same loop now, three weeks into her shifts at Gucci Osteria: from the pensione past Santa Croce's brick facade, across the river, looping back through the empty piazzas. Her lungs burned clean at kilometer four, calves tight from yesterday's twelve-hour close. Sweat traced paths down her temples, salt stinging her eyes. She pushed harder on the final stretch, the Mercato Centrale's iron gates coming into view just as the sky lightened to gold.

The market woke with her. Shutter doors rattled up, vendors hauling crates of tomatoes still dusted with garden soil, wheels rumbling over uneven stone. Sofia slowed to a walk at the entrance, chest heaving, wiping her face with the hem of her shirt. The air shifted thick—fresh bread baking in the forno downstairs, sharp citrus from lemon crates stacked high, the metallic tang of fish arriving in Styrofoam coolers. She made for her stall: old Giuseppe's corner, figs piled purple and split at the stem, pecorino wheels cut fresh with wire. "Buongiorno, Sofia," he called without looking up, knife slicing clean through a wheel, rind cracking crisp. "The usual?"

"Si, Giuseppe. Extra figs today." She handed over coins, fingers brushing his rough palm. He wrapped the pecorino in brown paper, figs loose in a paper bag that leaked faint sweetness. No longer the wide-eyed new girl fumbling change—he knew her run times, her preference for ripe figs that burst under thumb pressure. She ate standing at his counter: pecorino shaved thick with his pocket knife, paired with a fig split open, juice running sticky down her wrist. The cheese crumbled salty on her tongue, creamy against the fruit's floral give. Bystanders jostled past—nonnas with net bags, tourists snapping photos of prosciutto legs overhead—but here, amid the vendors' shouts and crate thuds, she fit. Breakfast done, she jogged home light, stomach settled warm.

Shower scalding quick, uniform on by 10:30. Black pants creased sharp, white shirt tucked precise, apron ties double-knotted. The walk to Gucci Osteria took fifteen minutes, past the Uffizi's morning crowds already queuing under umbrellas against a threatening drizzle. The garden entrance waited discreet: ivy thick on brick walls, buzzer humming her in. Kitchen clatter greeted her—pots banging steel counters, López calling orders in clipped Italian, the hiss of steam from blanching vegetables. "Sofia, station three," Marco directed, clipboard in hand. She moved to it fluid now: silverware aligned at thumb-width gaps, napkins folded into fans that stood alone, glassware checked for water spots under lamp light. No longer shadowing; she owned her prep.

Lunch service ramped fast at 12:00. The room filled with locals: businessmen in linen suits barking into phones, families from the suburbs claiming corner tables with gelato-smeared kids. Sofia worked table six first: four-top, suits from Milan, orders snapping out—risotto allo zafferano, grilled octopus, vitello tonnato. Tray loaded steady: plates balanced oval, thumb under edge, weight forward over hips. She shouldered the swing door, room humming—forks scraping porcelain, laughter rising from table two, sommelier decanting a Chianti with steady glug. No tilt today; she set plates clockwise from the hostess, ladle sides out. "Buon appetito." Nods all around, rhythm locked.

Peak hit at 13:15. Eight tables turning, kitchen pinging relentless: "Risotto per tavolo dieci—fuori!" Sofia grabbed her section's share—three bowls steaming gold, saffron threads vivid against creamy rice, parmigiano grated tableside. The businessman at table eleven waved her over impatient, tie loose, cheeks flushed red from the midday Barolo already half-gone. Mid-forties, paunch straining his shirt buttons, voice thick as he stabbed his fork into the risotto. "This is cold. Stone cold. You call this service?"

Sofia set the other bowls first, steady, then turned full to him. His wife shifted uncomfortable beside him, napkin twisted in her lap; the couple across exhaled quiet, forks paused mid-air. Room flow held—nearby tables glancing over, Marco circling distant. She met his eyes direct, voice even. "I'm sorry to hear that, sir. Tell me more—when did you notice it cooling?"

He leaned forward, breath heavy with wine. "The second bite. It's ruined. Send it back. And hurry—I have a train."

She nodded once, no interruption, palm up open. "I understand completely. Risotto should arrive steaming hot, and that must be frustrating after waiting. Let me make this right." No defensiveness, no rush to kitchen ping. His fork clattered plate, shoulders easing fraction as she held his gaze. "I'll comp a digestif for the table—amaro Nonino, on the house, to settle after the meal. And a fresh risotto inbound right now. Does that work for you?"

He blinked, bluster deflating. "Yeah. Fine. Amaro sounds good." His wife touched his arm light, relief plain. Sofia signaled kitchen subtle—fresh plate flipped fast, saffron blooming fresh under torch. Back with it piping, plus four glasses of amber amaro, ice clinking soft. "Here we are—hot through, and the digestifs." She placed clockwise, lingered second for his first bite. Chew, nod reluctant. "Better." Follow-up: "Everything good now?" Full table yeses, even a smile from the wife. Table turned happy, Marco's approving glance from the pass. Recovery clean—listen, empathize, comp, confirm. Avó's fire in guest form: push through the burn, make it right.

Service wound by 15:00, room emptying to a hum. Sofia cleared tables methodic: plates stacked right-side up to preserve sauce warmth, linens folded over arms. Debrief short: López grunted "Buon lavoro" over mise close, Marco noting her recovery for the book. Out at 15:45, feet throbbing familiar in clogs, but rhythm solid. No longer new; vendor nods at market, station owned, tables read clean.

Evening shift loomed at 19:00, but downtime first. Sofia biked to the osteria garden side entrance, sketchbook tucked in apron pocket. Dusk settled soft, lanterns flickering on as guests trickled in early. She claimed a bench by the lemon trees, pencils scratching quick: the garden wall's ivy curl, shadows pooling under tables, a waiter's silhouette pouring candlelit. Pages flipped—earlier sketches from runs: Mercato arches iron-laced, Ponte Santa Trinita's clean lines over Arno ripples, vendor hands slicing pecorino mid-motion. Details held the city: fig juice on thumb, cobble grit under sole, broth steam on cheek. No grand vistas; the small truths that stuck.

Paolo burst through the back door at 18:55, five minutes late as always, grin wide under Naples-dark curls. "Sofia! Save me a station?" He slung his bag, uniform half-buttoned, charm smoothing Marco's frown. Neapolitan through, always a story—last night, Vespa broke down near the duomo, flirted his way onto a stranger's. Maria arrived sharp, ponytail tight, eyes scanning the room like radar. From Peru, three years in Florence, tips queen—taught Sofia the up-sell whisper: "The Barolo elevates the vitello—shall I ask the sommelier?" Tonight's close promised drinks; they claimed their ritual spot, a hole-in-the-wall bar two blocks from Santa Croce, wooden stools scarred deep, walls papered yellow with faded posters.

Shift flew: twenty covers turned smooth, VIP anniversary table upsold seamless, no trays wobbled. Out at 23:30, trio biking tight pack through night streets, laughter cutting cool air. Bar door swung open to low jazz hum, air thick with grappa and old wood polish. Paolo ordered first: three Fernet con coca, glasses sweating condensation. "To Sofia—not dropping trays anymore," he toasted, clinking hard. Maria leaned in, voice low. "That businessman today? Perfect recovery. He left twenty percent." Sofia sipped bitter-sweet burn, warmth spreading chest. Paolo launched tales: Naples shifts dodging scooter gangs outside the restaurant, a guest proposing mid-pasta course. Maria countered quiet: "Lima was chaos—street carts flipping arepas at rush hour. Here, at least the plates match." Sofia listened, chiming in São Paulo memories—avó's kitchen feeding twenty neighbors from one pot. Bonds formed in shared ache: sore arches, turned tables, post-shift thirst. Midnight struck; they parted with hugs, Paolo insisting on her bike escort home.

Pensione quiet, room lit faint by courtyard lamp. Sofia kicked off clogs, feet red-imprinted, peeled socks slow. Sketchbook opened again: quick lines of Paolo's grin mid-toast, Maria's sharp gaze over glass rim. Homesickness hit then, unbidden—a hollow in her gut deeper than hunger. Avó's kitchen flooded back: São Paulo dusk filtering orange through slatted blinds, the wooden spoon clacking pot rim as sauce reduced. Sofia small, stirring onions translucent, avó's hand over hers. "Sempre com alma, menina. Soul in every turn." Voice gravel-rough, eyes crinkled warm. The phone waited on the bedside table; she dialed home, signal crackling faint over distance.

"Alô?" Avó's voice crackled through, pots clanging background.

"Avó, sou eu." Sofia curled on the bed, knees to chest.

"Sofia! Minha menina. Como vai Firenze?" Laughter bubbled, genuine.

"Bem. Mercado toda manhã, figs como você gosta. Turnei uma mesa hoje—risotto frio, mas recuperei." Words tumbled Portuguese-fast, details spilling: Giuseppe's knife, businessman's flush, Paolo's late grin.

Avó hummed approval. "Igual aqui—queimou o feijão hoje, mas ninguém reclamou. Você está crescendo, ouça. Orgulho." Pride thick in her tone, no lecture—just fact. "Volta quando puder. Beijos."

"Beijos, avó." Line clicked dead; Sofia held the phone warm against cheek, hollow filled fraction. Not alone—roots held across oceans.

Dawn run called early, but sleep pulled first. Sketchbook shut, light off. Florence rhythm settled bone-deep now.

Last drinks' bar echoes clung as she drifted: Paolo mentioning "servers who travel," eyes bright over grappa. "The circuit—Florence to Barcelona, Paris to London. Stagers everywhere, chasing stars. Disfrutar in Barca? Insane. Quimet i Quimet for tapas soul." Maria nodded: "Global kitchens. You next?" Sofia had smiled vague then. Now, in dark, the seed stirred—beyond Florence walls, more fires waited. Barcelona whispers: La Boqueria chaos, Gothic rain-slick stones. Not yet. But soon.
