How Tip-Out Systems Actually Work (And What Servers Should Know)
Tip sharing, also known as tip-outs, is one of the most common and least understood parts of working in a restaurant. While many servers know that a portion of their tips goes to other staff, few truly understand how these systems are structured — or how they can quietly reduce what you actually take home.
What Is a Tip-Out?
A tip-out is when servers are required to share a portion of their tips with other team members, such as bussers, hosts, bartenders, or kitchen staff. This practice is legal in many places, but the rules, percentages, and fairness of these systems can vary significantly from one restaurant to another.
Tip-outs are meant to recognize the team effort that goes into serving guests. However, when the system is poorly designed or overly aggressive, it can create frustration and financial strain for servers.

Tip Pooling Laws in the USA and Canada
Laws around tip pooling and tip sharing vary significantly between the United States and Canada.
United States
In the U.S., tip pooling is governed by federal law under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), though some states have stricter rules.
Key points:

Employers are not allowed to keep any portion of tips for themselves.
Only employees who “customarily and regularly receive tips” can be included in a mandatory tip pool.
In 2021, the U.S. Department of Labor updated its rules to allow back-of-house workers (such as cooks and dishwashers) to participate in tip pools — but only if the employer does not take a tip credit.
Some states (such as California, New York, and Massachusetts) have stronger protections and generally prohibit mandatory tip pooling with non-tipped employees.

Official Source:
U.S. Department of Labor – Tip Regulations
Canada
In Canada, there is no single federal law governing tip pooling. Rules are set at the provincial and territorial level, so they can vary depending on where you work.
Key points:

Employers are generally not allowed to take any portion of tips or gratuities for themselves.
Some provinces require employers to have a clear, written tip pooling policy.
Because the rules differ by province, it’s important to understand the specific laws in your area.

Why This Matters
Tip-outs directly affect your real hourly wage. A server who appears to be making strong money on paper can actually take home significantly less after tip-outs. Over time, this can create resentment, especially when the system feels unbalanced or when certain positions receive tip-outs without contributing equally to service.
Understanding how your restaurant’s tip-out system works helps you:

Make better decisions about where you work
Track your actual earnings more accurately
Identify red flags before accepting a new position

Questions Worth Asking
Before starting at a new restaurant, consider asking:

How is the tip-out calculated? (Tips or sales?)
What percentage goes to each position?
Are there any “ghost” positions that receive tips even when they’re not working?
How often do the rules around tip-outs change?

The Bottom Line
Tip-out systems aren’t going away, but understanding how they work puts you in a stronger position. The restaurants that retain good servers long-term tend to have transparent, fair systems. The ones that don’t often see high turnover and low morale.
