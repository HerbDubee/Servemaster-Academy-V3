    <h1 class="heading text-4xl lg:text-5xl font-bold leading-tight mb-6">Inclusive Service & Modern Guest Expectations</h1>
    <p class="text-zinc-400 text-lg leading-relaxed">Today’s guests expect more than good service — they expect to feel respected and seen. Here’s how to handle pronouns, accessibility needs, diverse backgrounds, and modern expectations with professionalism and warmth.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="prose">
    <p>Inclusive service isn’t about being politically correct. It’s about reducing friction and making every guest feel welcome without making assumptions about who they are or what they need.</p>

    <h2>Core principles of inclusive service</h2>

    <h3>1. Don’t assume — ask respectfully when needed</h3>
    <p>Instead of guessing someone’s gender or making assumptions about their relationship to the person they’re dining with, use neutral language until you have clarity.</p>
    <p><strong>Good phrasing:</strong> “What name would you like me to use?” or “How would you like me to refer to you?”</p>

    <h3>2. Use the language the guest uses</h3>
    <p>If a guest tells you their pronouns or preferred name, use them consistently. If you make a mistake, correct it quickly and move on without making a big deal out of it.</p>

    <h3>3. Make accessibility feel normal, not special</h3>
    <p>Offer assistance proactively but not intrusively. “Would you like me to bring the menu in a larger font?” or “Let me know if you need any help with the steps or the menu descriptions.”</p>

    <h3>4. Avoid “othering” language</h3>
    <p>Phrases like “for your kind of people” or “I don’t usually get customers like you” are well-intentioned but often land poorly. Treat every guest as an individual, not a representative of a group.</p>

    <blockquote>“The goal of inclusive service is simple: make every guest feel like they belong here — without drawing unnecessary attention to their differences.”</blockquote>

    <h2>Practical situations you’ll encounter</h2>
    <ul>
      <li>Guests who use they/them or neopronouns</li>
      <li>Guests with service animals (know the legal rules — you can only ask two specific questions)</li>
      <li>Guests who need modifications for mobility, vision, or hearing</li>
      <li>Guests from cultures with different dining customs</li>
      <li>Guests who are non-binary or transgender</li>
    </ul>

    <h2>The bottom line</h2>
    <p>Inclusive service is really just good service with fewer assumptions. The servers who do this well don’t have to be perfect — they just have to be willing to learn, listen, and adjust without defensiveness.</p>
